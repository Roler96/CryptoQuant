## Best Strategy: InsideBarBreakout

**Mechanism:** Close breaks previous bar's high/low + bar range > 1.5× ATR(14). Exit on reverse breakout.  
**Why it works:** The "1-bar lookback" uses yesterday's range as the breakout trigger — much shorter than 20-bar channel lookbacks, generating 66-263 trades. ATR expansion confirmation eliminates noise breakouts effectively.

**Breakeven analysis:** At 263 trades/year on ETH 1h, paying 5 bps round-trip per trade: total commission = 263 × 2 × 0.0005 × avg_position ≈ 2.6% annual drag. Gross Sharpe ~5.44 comfortably covers this. Commission stress at 10 bps still passes (Sharpe degradation minimal — strategy is not fee-sensitive).

InsideBarBreakout's 1-bar lookback generates 2-3× more trades while maintaining equal or better Sharpe. Shorter lookback = more breakout opportunities without adding noise (ATR filter handles that).

### Finding 2: INSIDE Bar Breakout > OUTSIDE Bar Breakout

Loop 5's RangeExpansionBreakout (outside bar: break 20-bar high/low) averaged 55 trades. InsideBarBreakout (inside bar: break prev bar high/low) averages 159. The inside-bar pattern is more frequent because it only requires ONE bar of compression (vs 20 bars of range establishment). The ATR expansion filter is equally effective on both patterns.

### Finding 3: CLV-Based Signals Too Sparse on Crypto

CLV+ATR Momentum failed 3/4 combos due to insufficient trades (6-29). CLV (Close Location Value) measures where the close sits within the bar's range — a subtle signal that fires infrequently in crypto's volatile price action. On ETH 1h, where it barely passed (39 trades, Sharpe=1.60), the edge is real but modest. CLV is better suited for traditional markets with cleaner open/close dynamics.
